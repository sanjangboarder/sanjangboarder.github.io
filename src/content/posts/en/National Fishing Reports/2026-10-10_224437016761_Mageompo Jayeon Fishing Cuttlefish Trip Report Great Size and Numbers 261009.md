Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

In early October, I had many trips caring for beginners, and TEAM MJT had another spontaneous group outing. I went cuttlefish fishing aboard the Jayeon Fishing Boat departing from Mageompo Port. As it was the beginning of the spring tide (Sari), the tidal currents were slightly fast, but the weather was probably the finest throughout this entire season.

Around 4:30 AM, I stopped by the tackle shop office to fill out the boarding register. They announced an early departure, so I headed straight down to Mageompo Port.

Being a weekend in early October with gorgeous weather, the place was bustling with crowds. Mageompo Port has now become widely loved as a top-tier port in Chungnam, attracting so many anglers that parking has become a genuine concern. Still, compared to other ports across the Chungnam region, it offers favorable conditions with relatively short distances between the parking areas and the boarding docks.

It feels like two months since I last visited here for white croaker fishing. Both the captain and the deckhand manager are always cheerful, witty, and kind, allowing me to board in high spirits.

For gear preparation, the captain instructed us to bring sinkers from size 18 to 25. In the Taean/Mageompo area, this sinker weight indicates that we would not be heading out to deep-water cuttlefish points around Sinjindo, Gungsido, or Ongdo. Recently, Sinjindo cuttlefish reportedly produced high counts but disappointing sizes, and since this day was Tide 5 (fast currents), venturing into deep-sea Gungsido waters would have meant dealing with overwhelming currents. Thus, I understood that we would primarily target Mageompo inner-bay waters, and the captain operated the boat points exactly according to that plan.

Therefore, I prepared setups geared toward detecting cuttlefish bites by tactile sensitivity rather than rod-tip visual monitoring in depths of 10 to 20 meters. I also arranged an egi rotation centered around proven cuttlefish favorites. Of course, because you never know what will hit on any given day, I always carry over 50 egis of various colors and models. Haha.

Setup 1, the AOMEX Navigator, is described by the manufacturer as having a 7:3 action, but as I noticed on my previous trip, it feels more like an 8:2 taper under a size 20 sinker. This action makes it versatile enough for tip-watching, but because it incorporates ultra-high modulus top-tier carbon—Toray T1100 and M40X—the tactile feedback delivered straight to the fingertips is exceptionally crisp. For Setup 2, I selected the Windwiser Idako with its stiff 9:1 action to focus purely on tactile weight-sensing techniques.

During the afternoon, we drifted mainly around points south of Geoyado Island. Strangely, cuttlefish would hit in rapid-fire bursts, followed by brief quiet spells, and then burst into action again—giving the distinct impression that the drifting boat was passing directly over tight clusters of cuttlefish schooling on the seabed.

Rather than aggressive, distinct thump strikes, more than half of the takes occurred either during lift-and-fall pauses (stay) or when gently dragging the egi across the bottom. I was stationed at the bow (front tower), but throughout the day the boat drifted in from the starboard stern, placing me in a somewhat unfavorable position. Casting out far and teasing them back with active rod work seemed to be what salvaged the bite for me.
