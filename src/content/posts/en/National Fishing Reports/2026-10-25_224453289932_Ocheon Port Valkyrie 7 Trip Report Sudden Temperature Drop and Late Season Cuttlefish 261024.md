Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

Last weekend, I headed out on a webfoot octopus and cuttlefish trip targeting the Taean waters aboard the Valkyrie 7 departing from Ocheon Port. This outing was the scheduled regular gathering for the Mankool Fishing Band. During the peak autumn cephalopod season, Ocheon Port bustles with lively energy from the earliest pre-dawn hours. We gathered with fellow band members who arrived punctually at the Valkyrie 7 berth, verified the passenger manifest, and made departure preparations.

The captain informed us we would be running a substantial distance offshore. Checking catch reports from the Mageompo fleet the previous day, I noticed a massive cuttlefish bite had exploded just south of Geoyado Island, so I anticipated we might head in that direction—and my prediction proved accurate.

After testing numerous octopus and cuttlefish rods throughout this season, my tackle choices have essentially distilled down to two primary setups. For heavier sinkers of size 18 and above, nothing matches the tactile feedback of the Aomax Navigator. For lighter sinkers in shallower waters, the Haedong Black Ink delivers superb balance. Thus, my tactical blueprint for the day was deploying the Navigator at deep cuttlefish spots requiring size 18 sinkers, and switching to the Haedong Black Ink at shallower octopus points with lighter sinkers.

It provides crisp tactile transmission that is hard to capture in words, and it will unquestionably accompany me on every remaining outing this season.

To state the conclusion upfront: fishing conditions throughout the day were exceptionally difficult. Reviewing catch reports across Incheon, Chungnam, and Gunsan fleets upon docking, numbers were uniformly subdued everywhere. Our primary destination was the cuttlefish ground south of Geoyado in Taean—the exact area where I had fished aboard the Jayeon Fishing Boat two weeks prior. Compared to that earlier success, managing even half that volume proved tremendously challenging today.

Consequently, it was a day characterized by non-stop egi rotations. Carrying around 40 egis in my tackle box, I cycled through almost every single pattern. Beyond the photos shown here, I tested far more lures than I could manage to document.

I firmly believe there is no such thing as an all-purpose universal egi for every situation. I occupied berth #2 on the boat. Rather than swinging the vessel heavily to favor the bow or stern, the captain drifted the boat evenly broadside, granting fair opportunities across both port and starboard. Success rested entirely on seizing opportunities when they arose, but cuttlefish bites were extraordinarily tentative, making tactile detection difficult even upon first presentation into unpressured zones.

Under such conditions, subtle rod-work is essential to amplify tactile feedback and coax cautious cephalopods. Rather than aggressive jigging, deliberate micro-movements were necessary to grind out bites one fish at a time. My neighbor 'Deline' even boated a commendable octopus, though everyone weathered a tough morning overall.

My final catch tally finished at 33 webfoot octopuses and 30 cuttlefish—ranking as my second-lowest haul of this entire season. While counts were humble, late-October individual specimens have grown noticeably hefty, yielding respectable overall biomass. The primary culprit for the slow bite was undoubtedly the midweek gales that churned bottom sediments combined with a sharp dip in water temperatures. As water temperatures approach 15°C, cuttlefish initiate their southward migration, causing hot spots to shift rapidly across the West Coast.

Water temperatures have now dipped below 20°C. The West Coast cuttlefish window likely has around two weeks remaining at best before focus turns back toward closing out the year with webfoot octopus. With autumn deepening, unpredictable weather and sudden cold snaps will make conditions increasingly technical.

Yet anglers don't fish only when conditions are idyllic. With approximately three voyages remaining until mid-November, I will continue reporting the latest West Coast cephalopod updates right to the season's close.
