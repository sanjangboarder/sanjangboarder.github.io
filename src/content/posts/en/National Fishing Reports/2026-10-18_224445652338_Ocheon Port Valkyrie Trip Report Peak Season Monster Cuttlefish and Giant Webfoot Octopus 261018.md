Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

Back in early September, right as the 2026 webfoot octopus and cuttlefish season opened, I visited Ocheon Port and achieved great results. It has been a little over a month since then, and to check how the peak-season fishing conditions at Ocheon Port are shaping up, I headed out once again aboard the Valkyrie. Ocheon Port likely hosts the largest fleet of fishing vessels in South Korea, which makes competition fierce and well-known charter fleets abundant.

When discussing renowned fleets for webfoot octopus and cuttlefish out of Ocheon Port, the Valkyrie is always at the top of the list. For this trip, I joined the Valkyrie operated by Ocheon Lure School, an official partner fleet of TEAM MJT. After signing the boarding manifest at the Ocheon Lure School office, we boarded right in front of the Fisheries Cooperative (Suhyup).

Because the sun rises later these days in mid-autumn, departure time was scheduled for around 5:50 AM. Sailing a bit later allowed me to get a full, restful sleep before heading out from home. People often say parking at Ocheon Port is notoriously difficult or the absolute worst, but having visited for over a decade, I have never once missed a boat due to parking issues. If you arrive about 20 minutes earlier than the vessel's notified reporting time—especially on weekends—there are plenty of private parking spots available for around 10,000 KRW, so there is no need to stress.

My operational plan was to deploy the Aomax Navigator in slightly deeper waters, and switch to the fast 9:1 action Windweiser Idako around the 10-meter mark. This was my second time taking out the Windweiser Idako, and it reaffirmed its undeniable strengths for anglers who favor crisp, extra-fast rods. Due to the rod's stiff characteristics, the weight of even a small octopus hanging on is amplified substantially, making it ideal for beginners or those struggling with bite sensitivity. Meanwhile, the Aomax Navigator was mainly used at spots 20 meters and deeper; thanks to its solid tip's delicate tactile feedback and recovery force, I could cleanly detect subtle, momentary cuttlefish wraps that would otherwise go unnoticed.

Morning fishing wasn't an explosive feeding frenzy, but rather a steady, rhythmic pickup one by one. My assigned position was right in the center of the starboard side; the bow and stern anglers pulled slightly higher headcounts, while the middle positions posted fairly balanced numbers. During boat repositioning breaks, I grabbed a hot cup of ramen, and before I knew it, the morning had flown by.
