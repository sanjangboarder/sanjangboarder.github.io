Earlier this year, there was a charter vessel fleet I had really been looking forward to boarding: the Rise out of Yeongjongdo. I had reserved this trip back at the beginning of the year, and finally got to head out. This season, anglers park in front of the Fishing School office and take a shuttle bus; we gathered at the office at 4:00 AM, and took a roughly 10-minute shuttle ride down to Jamjindo Wharf.

Jamjindo Wharf was teeming with an astonishing crowd—I honestly hadn't realized there were so many charter boats departing from here. It felt quite similar to waiting for boarding at Yeonan Pier. Because of the tidal dock height, only about two boats can dock simultaneously at the landing, so vessels must take turns pulling in. Despite arriving at 4:00 AM, parking directly at Jamjindo is virtually impossible, so shuttling in and waiting for turns meant we boarded the Rise around 5:00 AM. To top it off, a sudden passing shower drenched us haha, so the day began with quite an ordeal.

The Rise is a 7.93-ton commercial passenger fishing vessel with a capacity of 18 people. Reservations are capped at 17 anglers, and there was no deckhand onboard. It is a modern build roughly 2 to 3 years old, and the overall condition of the vessel was exceptional.

The bow casting deck is very spacious. When booking, assigning seats 1 or 2 allows anglers to fish from the elevated bow platform; I had seat #1, so I fished from the starboard bow. Every fishing station features a dedicated utility tray as shown below, making it remarkably convenient to rest beverages, egi lures, and tackle rigs.

We departed from Jamjindo cruising beneath the Muui Bridge. With rain falling, wind blowing, and temperatures chilly haha, it truly felt like cuttlefish season was heading toward its finale.

Because a gale-force wind had blown all day long yesterday, the sea water was completely turned over into murky brown mud water.

The pre-trip notice recommended sinkers between #14 and #20. Having spotted charter fleets clustering around the deeper southern zones of Jawoldo recently, my anticipation was that we would target deep water around Jawoldo; sure enough, the boat set course in that direction.

Anticipating deep water, I prepared my Aomax Navigator rod with #20 sinkers in mind. However, throughout the entire day, depths hovered primarily around 10 to 20 meters, so I ended up using a #16 sinker all day long.

The Aomax Navigator has been my primary workhorse rod for webfoot octopus and cuttlefish this season; for #16 to #20 sinkers, it suits my personal taste better than anything else. With the murky water killing overall feeding activity, bites were extremely delicate and finicky. The lures that triggered the most consistent reactions were the Yo-Zuri Tinsel L6 (Size 3.0) and the C-NEX Jjokkomi Green Fluorescent.

We began the morning casting close along the rocky shores north of Jawoldo, but with zero bites, the captain promptly relocated. We primarily targeted shallower flats on the south side of Jawoldo around the sandbar (puldung). A few cuttlefish surfaced across the boat here, but by that point I had only managed a single webfoot octopus... Total disaster haha. At that stage, I downgraded my daily ambition to just catching at least 10 cuttlefish.

Around 9:00 AM, the captain made another move directly over toward Jawoldo Wharf, which proved to be the absolute masterstroke of the day. Rather than hugging shoreline rocks, we drifted over a mud bottom slightly further out at a depth just over 10 meters until the early incoming tide began in the afternoon.

Here, cuttlefish finally began coming aboard. While the bite wasn't a frenzied blitz, decent-sized cuttlefish started appearing consistently. Although I missed a few hooksets, feeling solid cuttlefish tugging on the line instantly lifted my spirits. Static stays drew virtually no interest; subtle rod twitching or a gentle lift followed by a momentary pause triggered almost all of the strikes.

My morning score stood at 3 webfoot octopus and 18 cuttlefish. After early morning rain, the clouds vanished like magic and the weather turned pleasant... but the tough conditions could only be explained by the previous day's gale winds. Feeling quite worn out, I recharged my energy over lunch.

Until the early incoming flood tide began, we remained right around the Jawoldo Wharf spot. Initially, there were few other boats, but once word spread that squid were coming up, fleets from Incheon Yeonan Pier (Lukina, Lukina 2, Lukina 5, Goryeho, Boss) and Yeongheungdo (Myeongseongho) gathered to drift alongside us.

However, a noticeable difference between the Rise and other boats caught my attention. Our captain backed the boat into the wind with the stern facing the breeze. In contrast, the other vessels drifted sideways to the wind, mostly drifting along their starboard sides. The Rise subtly throttled the engine in reverse to regulate drift speed, drifting very slowly and methodically, while the other boats drifted by at a visibly faster pace.

Remarkably, the Rise steadily pulled up cuttlefish one by one, while other boats drifted past, found nothing clicking, and moved away. While bites seemed scarce elsewhere, fish were landing on our deck—a strong testament to our captain's precise boat control.

During the afternoon falling tide, several more cuttlefish came aboard.

As the incoming tide gained momentum, we relocated once again to the shallow sandbar flats on the south side of Jawoldo, but bites tapered off there.

Giving it my best effort until the final whistle, my final tally for the day came to 10 webfoot octopus and 36 cuttlefish. Given that most West Sea fleets reported catastrophic catch rates on this day and the following Saturday, I was quite pleased with this outcome.

The slight disappointment lay in the sizing: about 30% were nice large cuttlefish, 50% were modest cigarette-pack sizes, 10% were small, and the remaining 10% were tiny thumb-joint specimens that looked like they had just recently hatched. With November approaching, I had hoped for larger specimens, so I'll definitely have to plan a rematch.

The return journey from Jawoldo to Jamjindo Wharf takes a little over an hour. After taking a sound nap in the cabin, we docked safely just past 4:00 PM, and took the shuttle bus back to the office. Arriving home around 5:30 PM is truly the greatest perk of local Incheon boat fishing haha.

The strategic decision by the captain of the Rise to work the Jawoldo Wharf area paid off admirably, and it left me eager to board the vessel again under milder, clearer conditions. Booking may be tough for the rest of this year, but I'll reserve early for next season and share more updates.
