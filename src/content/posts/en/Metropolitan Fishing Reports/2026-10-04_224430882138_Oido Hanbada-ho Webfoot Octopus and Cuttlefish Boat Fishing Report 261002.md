Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

I went on a boat fishing trip targeting webfoot octopus (jjukkumi) and cuttlefish aboard Oido's Hanbada-ho. Departing from the midway dock along the Sihwa Seawall, Hanbada-ho was an operator I had never boarded before. It is a 9.77-ton high-speed vessel that normally operates morning and afternoon half-day schedules.

Parking space at the Sihwa Seawall midway dock is very limited, and depending on high tide, parked cars could even be submerged in seawater, so finding parking directly at the dock is virtually impossible unless you arrive the night before. In the past, shoulder parking was possible along the Sihwa Seawall roadway, but since parking bollards were installed and enforcement intensified 3 to 4 years ago, the system now requires parking near the tackle shop in the Steelland area and taking a shuttle bus to the dock.

Street parking in Steelland is free of charge, and being shuttled over means you can arrive right around departure time with peace of mind. However, because a single shuttle transports guests, each charter company operating from the midway dock has fixed shuttle departure slots. The schedule is adjusted periodically, but with fixed shuttle departure times, hardcore combat fishing extension hours are impossible; departures and returns are kept strictly on schedule. You can easily think of it as running the half-day schedule of Incheon South Port steel boats aboard a fast speed vessel.

Once each charter vessel docks at the midway pier, anglers board accordingly. Hanbada-ho is a relatively new build about 3 years old, and its deck and interior are generously spacious. It feels quite similar to Yeongjin-ho out of Yeongheungdo. In other words, it offers an exceptionally pleasant fishing environment. One unique detail is the presence of chairs on deck, something rarely seen on high-speed boats. You usually only see them on larger steel vessels, and here they seem to be a thoughtful amenity for beginners stepping into sea fishing on hourly trips.

As I detailed in an earlier dedicated review post, this Eustass rod purchased via AliExpress this year is truly unbeatable for octopus and cuttlefish. Even with a tuned baitcasting reel, the total combined weight of rod and reel is only around 150g. Anglers who pick it up are always taken aback by how featherlight it feels. I own many sets of gear, but after using this, going back to anything else requires readjustment. I think this might be the ultimate combo for webfoot octopus and cuttlefish.

On my last Incheon boat trip on Deokjeok-ho's morning run, I finished the entire outing using just Nude Gochujang and Military Green egis, but today required frequent egi rotations. That in itself shows that webfoot octopus activity was sluggish. With strong winds blowing for three consecutive days combined with a spring tide, the water clarity had turned extremely muddy. Whenever water conditions degraded even slightly, the octopus completely refused to climb onto the lures.

Perhaps due to the muddy water clarity, natural-toned egis showed clear dominance. The Haedong 3D printed egi proved effective in murky water, while Cinex's Silver Scale Anchovy egi, rigged on a dropper leader intended for cuttlefish, kept getting struck by webfoot octopus as well. Throughout the morning, these two lures played a pivotal role, and whenever the bite slowed down, I kept rotating through other colors.

Hanbada-ho operated mostly in shallow waters south of Palmido Island. During the early morning hours when winds were still relatively calm, catches were coming up steadily one after another, but the wind gradually began picking up.

While Gubongdo offered some relief from the wind, the water clarity there was at its absolute dirtiest. Things began getting tangled from this point on. Whenever bites ceased completely, the skipper relocated us to spots with slightly better clarity, but racking up numbers remained tough. I continued rotating through colors diligently in search of anything eliciting a positive response.

Around 11:00 AM, we had lunch. Thanks to the productive window before the wind picked up, quite a few webfoot octopus and cuttlefish had already accumulated in the live well. You could consider this equivalent to the tally of an entire morning half-day charter.

In the afternoon, the fierce wind caused water clarity to deteriorate everywhere. We pushed back out towards Palmido to fish, but the bite was far more subdued than in the morning. As always, once strong winds blow out the water, fishing loses its charm. The combination of daily gale-force winds over the preceding three days coupled with the spring tide seemed to have aggravated conditions even further.

My final tally was 159 webfoot octopus and 13 cuttlefish. The octopus had noticeably grown in average size, occasionally yielding late-November caliber specimens alongside small early-September sizes. The same went for cuttlefish: remarkably large ones were mixed with tiny palm-sized juveniles, reflecting the transitional nature of early October. Although the overall head count fell a bit short of expectations, their substantial bulk yielded 6 full vacuum-sealed packs, making it a satisfying outing.
