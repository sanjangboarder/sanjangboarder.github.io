Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

Following the previous day on Oido's Hanbada-ho, this was my second consecutive day of Incheon boat fishing. The previous day, I had led a private charter and finished exhausted without even knowing how the day flew by haha. The next day was the TEAM MJT official club outing, as well as the day to introduce my friend, CEO Kim, to boat fishing, with the goal of guiding him from absolute square one to shape him into a true angler.

Normally, going on back-to-back fishing outings two days in a row is far too exhausting, but due to overlapping schedules, I had no choice but to push through. To initiate CEO Kim, I prepared all the necessary equipment and gear for both of us myself.

Departing from Jindu Port on Yeongheungdo Island, Yeongjin-ho is quite renowned among Incheon boat fishing fleets and boasts top-notch vessel conditions. As early October arrived, the number of boats specializing exclusively in cuttlefish at Jindu Port grew tremendously. Arriving around 4:00 AM, I boarded, greeted fellow TEAM MJT club members, and began preparations.

We departed around 4:30 AM and navigated for about 30 minutes to begin near the waters overlooking the Yeongheungdo Power Plant. On the previous day's outing aboard Oido's Hanbada-ho, we had struggled due to murky brown water clarity, and knowing cuttlefish are notoriously sensitive to water clarity as well, I was naturally concerned.

For CEO Kim, who was making his sea fishing debut, rather than an ultra-light high-end setup, I configured a beginner-friendly combo featuring a Seahawk cuttlefish rod paired with a lightweight baitcasting reel. Throughout the day, he operated primarily with Yo-Zuri Tinsel #3.0 Watermelon and Gochujang colors, along with YL Military Green egis.

However, although I had scheduled the outing keeping in mind that I was bringing a complete novice, I only realized later that this was a dedicated cuttlefish charter, which caused some anxiety. Most would agree that boarding a dedicated cuttlefish boat on your very first day learning sea fishing is hardly ideal. After all, cuttlefish are trickier and yield far lower numbers than webfoot octopus. Still, since reservations were booked well in advance, we had to forge ahead, setting an initial goal of 10 cuttlefish.

I spent nearly an hour teaching him fundamental boat fishing etiquette, safety pointers, how to operate the rod and baitcasting reel, rigging mechanics, and the underlying principles behind working the lure. Being a sharp guy, he absorbed it rapidly. Once fishing commenced, I coached him on the crucial steps of feeling bottom contact and maintaining line tension to help him grasp the feel. To my astonishment, he landed around 6 cuttlefish during the morning session. Coming from the same graduate school lab with an engineering background, explaining the mechanics logically helped him execute smoothly and admirably haha. The morning was definitely a success.

While my friend performed far above expectations, I, on the other hand, was struggling considerably. Our assigned seats for the day were at the stern. At first, I was pleased thinking we would enjoy spacious and comfortable fishing, but the reality on the water turned out quite different haha.

This was a TEAM MJT club outing where I had seat #10, seats #1 to #8 were occupied by veteran MJT pros haha, and CEO Kim had seat #9 haha. To make matters worse, the water clarity was murky brown with sluggish bites, yet the boat drifted bow-first all day long haha. While overall numbers were hard to come by, the MJT pros at the bow swept up the bites ahead of us almost the entire day haha.

I was stuck with trickling single catches haha. I tried casting out and dragging the lure back, experimenting with various adjustments, but struggled heavily. It was a humbling reminder of just how intimidating it is when top-tier veterans at the bow don't miss a single bite. For the occasional chances that came my way, the Cinex Silver Scale Anchovy egi elicited the most reactions; it truly seems effective when water clarity is poor.

Over the past few days, boats had avoided heading near Jawoldo Island due to poor cuttlefish response caused by strong winds and spring tides, but seeing bites around the Yeongheungdo Power Plant prove lackluster as well, the skipper decided to make a move.

For the afternoon session, we drifted mainly through channel waterways around Jawoldo Island at depths of 15 to 25 meters. There was a 30-minute window when the boat's drift shifted diagonally rather than pushing purely bow-first haha. Capitalizing without hesitation, I pulled up 7 consecutive hits of decent-sized cuttlefish haha.

Indeed, with 8 expert anglers enticing bites at the bow, opportunities simply weren't trickling down to me. For about an hour with mixed side-drifts, I managed to recover some numbers. As depths increased, instances of hooked cuttlefish coming unbuttoned also grew more frequent.

With numbers already running low, having hooked fish slip away was truly exasperating.

After that brief favorable current, the drift returned to pushing straight from the bow haha. I cleared my mind and tried casting out, but results remained modest. Since I didn't attach an egi directly beneath the sinker, webfoot octopus catches were minimal and the bag was filled almost entirely with cuttlefish. While a few small juveniles surfaced here and there, the vast majority were decent, usable sizes.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjEwMDRfODgg/MDAxNzkxMDgyNzk5MTEz.oO1Fu399UgxLbaZvB6gFA6QFFAPXXZmFBiW5j3qia5Eg.LxTJxVBl2Ehw0OcIDzsieyyvk_bKUuPx_WMGQ19sI9Ig.JPEG/SE-6deff7aa-bf83-11f1-9454-23b28a50735b.jpg?type=w800)

My final cuttlefish fishing score stood at 10 webfoot octopus and 26 cuttlefish. Because there were quite a few sizeable specimens, the overall volume ended up fairly substantial, though it was impossible not to feel a lingering regret over the head count.

The top angler at the bow on this day scored in the high 50s for cuttlefish, meaning I caught about half that tally. Yeongjin-ho wraps up lines promptly at 3:00 PM and docks right on time. While hardcore combat fishing is great, arriving home early to spend the evening with family is something my household deeply appreciates, providing a welcome justification for metropolitan fishing trips.

My previous octopus and cuttlefish trip on Yeongheungdo's Yeongjin-ho brought great catches thanks to favorable weather and tide conditions. On this trip, while the tide was suitable, murky water clarity and wind led to a somewhat modest harvest. While perfect conditions every time would be ideal, the unpredictability of sea fishing is precisely what keeps the sport so intriguing. Fortunately, everyone had an enjoyable time sharing great memories during the club outing and took home plenty of fresh catches to feast on, making this official meet another success.

After a day of rest, I will be heading out again on the substitute holiday to introduce two more beginners to sea fishing—this time aboard Kukje Yuseon's Happy-ho out of Incheon Namhang Port—and share the story while guiding them. This beginner introduction was a resounding success; if you are curious to see whether our next outing with two female anglers turns out equally successful, stay tuned and come visit again.
