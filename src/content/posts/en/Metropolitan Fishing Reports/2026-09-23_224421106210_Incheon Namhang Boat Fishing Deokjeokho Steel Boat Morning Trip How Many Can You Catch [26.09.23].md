Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

This morning, I went out for webfoot octopus and cuttlefish fishing on the morning charter boat Deokjeokho operated by Deokjeok Sea Fishing, departing from the renowned Incheon Namhang Recreational Fishing Pier. Initially, I intended to board Happyho of Gukje Yuseon as I usually do, but no spots were available. So I opted for a less familiar charter company just for a quick morning session. Let me share how it went!

To board Deokjeokho, you fill out the passenger manifest at the Deokjeok Sea Fishing office first. Perhaps because they operate only one boat—Deokjeokho—the office is not very large. What caught my eye was that the rental reels were pink Banax reels; that morning, the anglers to my left, right, and all around me were rocking pink gear (haha). That shows just how many beginners and first-timers visit for hands-on experience fishing.

In hopes of securing a good spot on the steel boat, I arrived early. The photo below shows what it looked like around 4:10 AM. Prior to this hour, full-day steel boats and 9.77-ton express boats occupy the boarding pontoons; once they depart, the hourly half-day boats dock at the barge from precisely this time onward.

Here is Deokjeokho. It is a relatively newly built vessel only a few years old. Although traditionally referred to as a "steel boat", it appears to be built of FRP. It looks as though it was built from the exact same hull mold as Yongjin 3 on Yeongheungdo Island.

Departure took place around 6:20 AM; it seemed some anglers arrived a bit late, making our boat the last one to leave the pier. One major hallmark of Incheon boat fishing this year has been solid catches of webfoot octopus and cuttlefish near Incheon Bridge. As expected, the fishing grounds spanned from the front of Incheon International New Port to Incheon Bridge, located only 10 to 20 minutes away.

Since it was a morning half-day trip, I brought a spare rod, but it was purely a backup and I used a single rod the entire session. The tide was 4-mul, right after the golden tide window when current begins picking back up, with a tidal range around 400—a reasonably decent condition. Seeing that most surrounding anglers were using octopus sinkers, I matched my setup with a No. 14 sinker.

The water depth was under 10 meters, making the physical fishing straightforward, and both weather and tide were decent, yet it wasn't an easy bite. In that 1.5-hour span, I had landed 31 octopuses. Having set a goal of 100 for the trip, I started feeling slightly uneasy.

Cuttlefish also hit the deck periodically, though not in high frequency. This was my 5th trip of the season, and I think I met my largest cuttlefish of the year here, alongside smaller ones as well.

Incheon Namhang morning charter boats typically dock back at the pier between 11:00 AM and 11:10 AM. That means when fishing near Incheon Bridge, rods are reeled up around 10:30 AM. Actual fishing time lasts about 3.5 hours, or 4 hours at most. You don't experience a full tidal cycle, only catching roughly two-thirds of it. Fortunately, low tide was around 9 AM on this day, allowing us to hit both the tail end of the ebb tide and the early flood tide, which worked in our favor.

When I got home, it was precisely 11:20 AM. After a sweet, relaxing afternoon nap, I am now writing and sharing this trip report (haha).
