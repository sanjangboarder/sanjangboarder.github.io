Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

On Monday following the Chuseok holiday, our company had a recommended leave day. Wondering what to do, I thought boat fishing for webfoot octopus and cuttlefish might be tough due to the spring tide. Since I had also scheduled car maintenance for the morning, I decided to take a short, quick session to Eurwangni, which had been generating buzz for shore octopus and cuttlefish.

For parking, you can use the Eurwangni public parking lot. Perhaps because it was a Monday, it was completely deserted and empty.

From the parking lot to the Eurwangni rocky shore point is about 1.5 km. If you traverse the small hill from there, it comes to just under 2 km, so you can reach it with a relaxed 20- to 30-minute walk.

Personally, this was my first time visiting Eurwangni Beach for fishing. The beach had already wrapped up its summer season, and being a weekday, it was exceedingly quiet. Low tide on this day was around 12:00 PM. I arrived just before 11:00 AM, so the tide had already receded substantially.

Beyond the beach area, the walking trail heading towards the rocky shore was very well constructed. Because the direct sunlight was intense, I walked fully geared up with a neck buff, arm sleeves, and sunglasses. After walking for about 15 minutes, the first spot—a pier dock—came into view, and even on a weekday, many anglers were already casting for octopus and cuttlefish.

Therefore, I decided to cross the hill to reach deeper along the rocky shoreline. Rather than a strenuous mountain hike, it is just a small wooden staircase path over a low knoll, so the difficulty is minimal. You can reach the inner rocks in about 5 minutes.

For my octopus and cuttlefish setup, I brought out the Red Pulse GP863ML, an all-round inshore rod making its maiden voyage on this day. I also debuted my Shimano Sephia XR paired with the Gomexus double handle, and the combo felt superb. In particular, user reviews of the Red Pulse 863ML are quite scarce, but fishing with it showed a crisp, sharp recovery typical of high-elasticity carbon alongside impressive sensitivity. I felt its true character would shine even clearer when casting metal jigs, but I hadn't packed a single metal lure, unfortunately. Baitfish happened to swarm right in front of the rocks, so missing that chance was a bit regrettable!

I grabbed whatever egis and sinkers I had lying around at home, but overall, bites from octopus and cuttlefish were sluggish. Word had it that catches were prolific in early September, but by now it felt like gleaning leftovers after the main harvest had passed.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjlfMjE2/MDAxNzkwNjU4ODc2ODY2.d14gEHfkEbiNY0D-T0ZCnE5h880lSFj8N0YlLjaeducg.rhJiAQivV79NGJdqmAWPRV2nharAi0UcHQGt2PFi9Ukg.JPEG/900_20260928_112946.jpg?type=w800)

Despite being a weekday, there were quite a lot of anglers along the Eurwangni rocks. I managed to squeeze into a vacant pocket, but water depth right in front was quite shallow. Even though it was a spring tide with extreme ebb, I wondered whether fishing during a higher tide stage might have yielded better water depth and results.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MzBfMTgz/MDAxNzkwNzQwMDM3NjA2.Vb93ejZXFmewRo3aH_Iv7E9QgopkjWHzGxbE9S0r-bUg.sEZejVDVOW5PPAwVWsY_xz9d7kos4ankRWPck75wsLQg.JPEG/SE-a612a620-bbc4-11f1-9483-8db9cbb0c3b2.jpg?type=w800)

The weather was gorgeous, yet neighboring anglers were rarely seen landing anything. Whether due to thinned numbers or low activity, the quiet action proved a bit frustrating. I fished for about 1 hour and 30 minutes, staying just long enough to see the initial push of the rising tide before packing up.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MzBfMjc2/MDAxNzkwNzQwMDUwNzAw.uuthoBAbS-FKdEogRtiDWUbwgVACNEBok2eqD1Npftkg.FktRlXyYdriZGDuNIPgnrvUUhmC9MkN7BgvMyRjxtzog.JPEG/SE-a6182463-bbc4-11f1-9483-c519c79bcec2.jpg?type=w800)

While racking up numbers was difficult, I luckily managed to hook one cuttlefish on the Wangnun-i Nude Gochujang egi, along with about 5 webfoot octopuses. The cuttlefish hit right as the lure approached the rock pile directly in front, whereas the webfoot octopus struck out at long casting distance.

Perhaps because it had been a while since I last fished from coastal rocks, fatigue set in quickly, and with the tide rushing back in, sustaining a prolonged session proved difficult. Anyone planning to visit the Eurwangni rocks should aim to arrive at least 2 hours prior to low tide to ensure a comfortable window for octopus and cuttlefish fishing.

Another long holiday weekend approaches in early October, during which I will be heading out on three consecutive boat trips in the Incheon area guiding beginners. The trips include a friend boarding a boat for the first time, a group beginner guide outing, and a half-day charter with two absolute newcomers. In my upcoming reports, I will share the results of those octopus and cuttlefish outings with beginner anglers.
