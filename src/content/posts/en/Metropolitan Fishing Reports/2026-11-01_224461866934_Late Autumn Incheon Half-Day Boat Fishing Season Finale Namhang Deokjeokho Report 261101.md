Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

​

Back in early October, I embarked on three consecutive trips to introduce complete beginners to Incheon webfoot octopus (jjukkumi) fishing. Following up on that, the newcomers enjoyed it so much that they asked for a second round, so I once again took them out to Namhang Wharf, the renowned sanctuary for beginner boat fishing experiences in Incheon.

Taking a morning half-day boat lets you park conveniently within the pier's dedicated lot, and because morning vessels return to dock around 11:00 AM, it is an accessible, low-pressure way for novice anglers to experience squid fishing. Honestly, taking beginners straight onto high-speed planning hulls can be overwhelming, so this style is truly ideal. At Incheon Namhang, there are about 10 large 40-to-60-passenger steel vessels registered as excursion fishing cruisers. An overwhelming majority run half-day morning schedules, which is why this port has naturally evolved into a beginner paradise.

Last time, we boarded the Happy on Gukje Yuseon, but because Gukje is so popular, reservations were already completely filled three weeks prior. Fortunately, the Deokjeokho—which had left a great impression on me as a modern, clean build when I fished it solo—had plenty of open slots, allowing me to secure spots easily.

Namhang morning boats generally cast off around 6:20 AM. That remained unchanged, but today I noticed that full-day fast cruisers departed considerably later. In October, full-day boats pulled out around 4:00 AM, allowing half-day boats to berth early; now with late sunrises, they were heading out around 5:00 AM. As late autumn sets in, the sky only brightens properly around 7:00 AM.

From mid-October onwards, sinker weights of #16 to #20 are standard; around the Songdo flats and Incheon Bridge points, a #16 sinker works perfectly regardless of the tide, so I brought my familiar setup. I also packed tackle for the two beginners: a JS Company Liberty Ocean and a Fishing Korea cuttlefish rod. With rain and wind throughout the trip, line wraps occurred causing tips to bend, but entry-level rods proved remarkably forgiving and easy to handle in such situations.

Today, the webfoot octopus showed strong responses to the classic Incheon excursion combo: Nude Gochujang and Military Green. At dedicated cuttlefish points, I switched off that combo and deployed the Tinsel paired with C-NEX Watermelon Laser, a tactic that yielded great results.

As seen in the photos, rain poured all morning long—and persisted right into the afternoon. Having a full waterproof rain suit, I was completely fine regardless of wind and downpours, but because most guests were casual experience anglers, many undoubtedly had a chilly time. Reports from the preceding couple of days across Incheon had shown abysmal catch rates, so I didn't set my hopes too high. To my surprise, however, webfoot octopus bit at a steady cadence.

All Namhang half-day vessels provide complimentary snacks. Usually tteokbokki is served, but Deokjeokho dished out piping-hot fishcake soup (odeng-tang), with very generous portions. In cold, wet weather, that warm soup tasted like pure nectar. Beating the chill, we kept accumulating octopus in our livewells, with cuttlefish making intermittent appearances.

Soaked through by the rain, our appearance was somewhat disheveled, but the two beginners—who had aimed for 10 catches each—massively exceeded expectations: one landed 27 and the other 20. Having mentored so many beginners this season, I wonder if I might actually have a bit of a knack for coaching newcomers. Looking around the deck after lines up, most guests averaged around 5 catches, with top anglers hitting around 20, so guiding second-time beginners to this level felt genuinely rewarding haha.

My personal final count was 36 webfoot octopus and 5 cuttlefish. While the total number was modest, the cuttlefish were bulky, allowing me to pack two full freezer bags.

At 11:00 AM, the boat cruised back at full throttle; in fact, due to the adverse rainy weather, the captain brought us into port a few minutes before 11:00. Under such rough conditions, pushing further made little sense, so docking slightly early felt rather sensible haha.

In all respects, this was another highly successful beginner mentoring outing in Incheon. They had so much fun that they expressed interest in trying high-speed fast cruisers next season; we'll see about that next year haha!

​

Over the past weekend, cephalopod catches across Incheon and Chungnam were generally very tough. As autumn deepens, it feels like time to gently bid farewell to the webfoot octopus and cuttlefish that brought us joy over the past two months. Nevertheless, I still have two more scheduled outings booked, so I'll continue reporting live conditions from the water through late November.
