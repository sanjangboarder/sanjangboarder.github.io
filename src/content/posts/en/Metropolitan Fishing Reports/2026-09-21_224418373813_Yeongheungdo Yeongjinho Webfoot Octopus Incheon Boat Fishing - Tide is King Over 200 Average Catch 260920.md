Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

One of the most noticeable trends in the 2026 Yellow Sea boat fishing season is the tremendous surge of the Incheon area during this webfoot octopus season. Catches that easily rival the Chungnam region—traditionally recognized as the mecca of webfoot octopus—are consistently coming in. In particular, while Incheon faces tougher conditions than Chungnam during spring tides (strong current periods), when looking at the golden neap tides from "Musi" (slack tide) to 3-mul under similar conditions, one might even wonder if Incheon is actually performing better.

Having already completed three outings in Chungnam, this time I headed out on Yeongjinho at Yeongheungdo in the Incheon area for an official regular club outing with our TEAM MJT members. Autumn weather often brings unfavorable conditions, but having sunny skies aligned with the golden 1-mul tide is genuinely rare. We truly picked the perfect day.

Visiting Jindu Port after quite some time, the place felt familiar as ever. Parking seemed to have become more challenging compared to last year, but the boarding area where the high-speed charter boats are docked was neatly organized, which was great to see.

Yeongheungdo Yeongjinho is a newly built vessel constructed around 2023, and among 9.77-ton high-speed boats, it ranks among the exceptionally spacious ones. Even within the same 9.77-ton category, the resting areas, snack areas, and passageways for moving along the deck are remarkably roomy, allowing for a much more comfortable fishing experience.

In the early morning dawn, I greeted our club members and enjoyed a hot cup of ramen since it was available.

We set sail before 5:00 AM. Nowadays, sunrise doesn't brighten the sky until around 6:30 AM, so departing excessively early doesn't make much sense anyway. Since webfoot octopuses rarely bite well in total pitch darkness, leaving at this hour felt like perfect timing.

Here is the gear and tackle setup prepared for the day. In the morning, we fished in depths of 10–15 meters, and in the afternoon, we moved into 25–30 meter depths. Because the tide was so calm and favorable, I operated all day with a single #12 tungsten sinker, and the egi lure rotation was not excessively frequent.

The webfoot octopus rod and baitcasting reel together totaled roughly 150g, creating an ultra-lightweight setup. A considerable number of octopuses were landed on this day, and thanks to this lightweight configuration, the fatigue on my fingers and wrists remained completely manageable. If I had used conventional heavy gear, I would have suffered quite a bit of ache afterward.

These were the egis that participated in the morning rotation. The best combination proved to be the Yeollin Nude Red Head paired with the Military Olive color. It performed solidly before sunrise and continued well after the sun came up. During slow intervals in between, my trusted go-to lures of this year—the Chokkomi Fluorescent Green and Horse Mackerel colors—were just as remarkably productive. Additionally, while I was primarily targeting webfoot octopus in the morning, seeing plenty of cuttlefish being caught prompted me to deploy the Haedong 3D Printing Egi and Yo-Zuri Tinsel. Strangely, the Yo-Zuri Tinsel did not work at all, whereas the Haedong 3D Printing Egi brought up both webfoot octopus and cuttlefish very reliably.

With a golden tide, slight breezes yet mirror-calm "glass water" surface conditions, and marvelous weather, it was easily the single best set of conditions across all four outings this season for webfoot octopus fishing.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjFfMjcy/MDAxNzg5OTQ5NzU0NDc3.VtFG1v_nzYrp3KJZRdpvL7jQY8GB-IErvKPyThzDrLQg.I5tXdueSibLSekGA7JAVtS8HQgq_dtlwTVkzu68RlW0g.JPEG/SE-8f90d6cd-b4cf-11f1-b999-13d0a5259bea.jpg?type=w800)

During the morning session, the webfoot octopuses came up almost non-stop without any lull. Their activity level was so intense that I could even describe it as overly active. On poor activity days, octopuses come up limp or hooked by just a single tentacle tip, but here countless octopuses were gripping the egi tightly right to the end, showing tremendous vitality.

After targeting mainly webfoot octopuses, seeing cuttlefish bite actively prompted me to intentionally lift the sinker slightly off the seabed on my branch line rig. Yet webfoot octopuses pounced on it endlessly, haha. By around 10:00 AM, my counter recorded 124 webfoot octopuses and 24 cuttlefish. Quite a solid score! At that pace, breaking 300 catches seemed well within reach.

The culprits were the Yo-Zuri Tinsel and HRM Watermelon Laser... Strangely, neither webfoot octopus nor cuttlefish would latch on. Restoring my morning pace was only possible after having lunch.

Lunch on Yeongjinho out of Jindu Port, Yeongheungdo, is served buffet-style. The self-service system where you take as much as you like is wonderful. With octopuses and cuttlefish being caught in solid numbers, the meal went down delightfully well.

Boat fishing fleets generally fall into two styles: hard-core combat fishing boats and on-the-dot finish boats, haha. Yeongjinho belongs to the prompt wrap-up style, finishing right at 3:00 PM sharp. Arriving back at port usually takes until about 4:00 PM. Being in the Incheon area and close to home, returning early was so pleasant, haha.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjBfMTc3/MDAxNzg5ODkzOTAzMDAw.vlzhPXr4Sc1g7msNud6PWoAgaAPCZC33a5ag0xz6x2Ig.Z8zMVHpGG2PC7k5lmlAvWFGZp13ksXboij2C31isu1Mg.JPEG/900_20260920_144820.jpg?type=w800)

Tallying up my final catch gave 191 webfoot octopuses and 71 cuttlefish. The Eustace AliExpress rod I used on this day operated with superb sensitivity. Catching the bites and hooking octopuses must have comfortably exceeded 300, but I easily lost over 50 during retrieval. Since it is a soft-action rod, there was noticeable swaying and flexing during the fight, which seemed to have contributed to spit hooks, alongside my gentle hooksets in deep water potentially leading to shallow hook penetration. In any case, had I reduced the lost catches by even half, crossing 300 would have been effortless, leaving a slight touch of lingering regret.
