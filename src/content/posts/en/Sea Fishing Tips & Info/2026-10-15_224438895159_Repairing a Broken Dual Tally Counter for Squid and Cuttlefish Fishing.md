Since the push button—the most vital component of a tally counter—was broken, I wondered if I should just toss it in the trash. But wondering if it could be fixed, I decided to take it apart. Disassembly turned out to be remarkably simple. As you can see below, unfastening the screws allows it to come apart very easily.

Structurally, detaching just the push button looked feasible, but one wrong slip risked scattering the internal number wheels, which would make reassembly quite a headache. That made me realize replacing the entire inner counter assembly altogether would likely be much simpler.

The internal counter mechanism from the single tally counter detached very easily. However, the one issue was that the reset knob to reset back to "0000" was located on the opposite side. To my surprise, the reset knob shaft could easily be removed and reinstalled onto the other side. As shown in the pictures below, you can simply pull off the knob and pop it onto the opposite side without hassle.

When I transplanted this unit into the dual counter housing, it slotted in as if it were the factory original component. There was zero tolerance mismatch, proving that the internal mechanism is 100% identical. Cheap single clicker counters cost only around 1,500 KRW, so the repair cost practically nothing!

A new dual tally counter costs well over 10,000 KRW—roughly the price of nearly ten single clickers—making it a clever and useful novelty product. Since the dual outer housing cannot be purchased separately, buying one is worthwhile, and whenever the internal counter mechanism breaks, rusts, or jams, you can simply grab a budget single tally counter and swap the innards.

Since this product has been on the market for several years now, many anglers might run into similar wear and tear issues. If your counter ever breaks down, I highly recommend trying this quick DIY maintenance!
