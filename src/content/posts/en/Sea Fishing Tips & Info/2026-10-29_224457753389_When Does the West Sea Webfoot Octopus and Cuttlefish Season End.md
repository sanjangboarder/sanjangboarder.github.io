Now that mid-October has passed, it feels like the webfoot octopus (jjukkumi) and cuttlefish (gap-ojingeo) boat fishing season is steadily drawing toward its close. Indeed, even on weekends, some charter boat fleets now show occasional vacant spots, and booking weekday trips has become noticeably easier. The prevailing atmosphere seems to signal that the peak season is nearing its tail end and anglers should soon prepare to wait for next year.

When exactly does the most popular boat fishing season in the West Sea—webfoot octopus and cuttlefish—actually conclude? To understand this properly, one needs a solid understanding of the biological characteristics of both species. While both are peak autumn targets, they exhibit completely different ecological behaviors.

​

First, webfoot octopus (*Amphioctopus fangsiao*) can easily be understood as an indigenous, resident species. As winter approaches and water temperatures plummet, they do not migrate south toward warmer waters; instead, they overwinter right in the very local waters where they were born. Much like a bear hibernating through winter, webfoot octopus burrow into muddy flats or shells in their native habitat to pass the cold season. Consequently, anglers can continue targeting them even down to the lower water temperatures where their activity significantly slows. That workable period generally extends until early December, and although results vary under different maritime conditions, I have personally experienced them reacting to lures even down to water temperatures of around 6°C.

In stark contrast, cuttlefish (*Sepia esculenta*) are migratory cephalopods that travel actively in pursuit of warmer waters. In the West Sea, water temperatures begin cooling gradually starting from Incheon in the north, cascading through Chungnam, Gunsan, Mokpo, and eventually into the South Sea. Anglers can consider that cuttlefish initiate their southward migration once the local water temperature dips to around 15°C.


<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjEwMjlfMjk2/MDAxNzkzMjQ4NTE5NjE4.i96fYX8DQl9WO_eh28PY9UEF1CjEXcpRGo5lbmKgMjQg.Ds5lhxF2BhrMAyWjBhz5h3jzTSJCrkkQPOn6dqKxVPEg.JPEG/43017.jpg?type=w800" />
</div>

In other words, rather than arbitrarily picking mid-November or late November on a calendar, tracking real-time water temperature drops is the most accurate way to anticipate the season's finale. Looking at historical Incheon water temperatures, in 2025 water temperatures reached 15°C around mid-November, whereas the year before, it only reached 15°C toward late November. Broadly speaking, it is accurate to say that the cuttlefish season concludes starting from the northern Incheon sector between mid-to-late November.

Examining the cuttlefish migration route with Incheon as the starting point, they travel south past Taean and Boryeong in Chungnam, down past the Anma Archipelago, Imjado, and Heuksando in Jeolla Province.

Once they reach Mokpo, Wando, and Goheung, they linger briefly along select coastal pockets of the South Sea before venturing further out toward Geomundo and Jeju waters to spend the winter in deep offshore waters.

This is why, starting from mid-November, wholesale fish markets at major fishing ports such as Gunsan or Hongwon Port often see an abundance of cuttlefish being unloaded. Commercial fishermen install trap nets directly along the migratory corridors where cuttlefish head south, landing them in tremendous volumes.

To summarize everything clearly, I created an informational illustration with Gemini:

Webfoot octopus are resident species that do not migrate far away; as water temperatures drop, their metabolic activity slows down and they enter a state of winter dormancy. Because of this adaptability, targeting them remains viable even until early December. On the other hand, cuttlefish instinctively migrate toward warmer southern regions once water temperatures decline toward approximately 15°C. Thus, across the West Sea, cuttlefish boat fishing draws to a close centered around mid-November with some regional variation.

This suggests we have roughly two weeks remaining in the cuttlefish season. Of course, cuttlefish do not mechanically measure exactly 15°C to depart all at once, so individual perception varies and minor shifts occur; please understand that the main peak season is essentially winding down. After mid-November, webfoot octopus fishing will still remain viable, so I hope cephalopod boat anglers find this overview helpful when planning their final trips of the 2026 season.
