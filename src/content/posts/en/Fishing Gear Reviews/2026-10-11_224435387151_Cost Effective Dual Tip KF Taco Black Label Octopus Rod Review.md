Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

This year's West Sea common octopus (rock octopus) boat season appears to be finally getting underway. While catches were sluggish last year and early this season, reports of octopus catches have started emerging from Chungnam and Gunsan waters as we move into October. Although action isn't explosive, targeting favorable tides should reliably secure baseline numbers. I am also currently planning an octopus outing for early November.

In preparation for rock octopus charters across both the West Sea and South Sea, I looked into dedicated octopus rods. KOREA FISHING (KF), which has been rolling out a wide range of new fishing tackle this year, recently launched a budget-friendly common octopus rod, so I picked one up.

The product name is the TACO BLACK LABEL, and let's examine it in detail piece by piece. For an additional 15,000 KRW, you can bundle it with a dedicated rod case. The case lists the model as "C150XH/160XH." You might wonder why two model designations appear on a single product: the package uniquely includes two interchangeable Section 1 tips.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjEwMDVfMTY5/MDAxNzkxMTk1NTEwODM2.4NGYWHZwH_qRD6dOdzmaUyhPWfuQ1DuhfjSu_vkCY1sg.UEg-p0AGEdlwkGyequr5QosOdTjNRdrdo-UxJaC33jog.JPEG/SE-774dcdc4-bd8a-11f1-af42-c95a3af504a9.jpg?type=w800)

Reviewing the contents, there is a hard rod case (optional add-on), a cloth protective sleeve, a rod belt, one butt section, and two separate Section 1 tip blanks.

Because octopus boat fishing places tremendous strain on tackle—often risking rod breakage when prying stubborn octopuses off reefs—carrying a spare rod offers great peace of mind. With the KF Taco Black Label supplying two tip sections out of the box, you essentially don't need to haul a separate backup rod.

The KF Taco Black Label pairs one butt section with two interchangeable tip blanks. One tip section is roughly 10 cm longer than the other, yielding distinct actions. When the tip length extends, the upper section flexes slightly more gracefully; hence, while classified as 150XH and 160XH, the 160XH delivers a slightly more supple feel.

Octopus rods generally split into two design philosophies: shaving weight to the absolute edge of barely holding on, or prioritizing brute pulling power without worrying about delicate limits. The KF Taco Black Label leans decisively toward the latter. Rated with a formidable 20 kg breaking strength, it delivers colossal lifting power. While its self-weight naturally lands in the 140 g bracket, clever balance tuning effectively alleviates felt weight during actual fishing.

Observing the 150XH balance, the fulcrum sits right in front of the reel seat. Once a baitcasting reel is mounted, the center of gravity aligns directly over the reel seat, ensuring minimal hand fatigue compared to its dead weight.
