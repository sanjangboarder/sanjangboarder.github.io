Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

Numerous new egi lures have hit the market this year, and I have brought along another hidden gem that deserves more attention. It is the ROI BB egi released by Haedong Tackle. Although compact in size, it elevates overall build quality substantially, focusing on the most sought-after fish-attracting colors.

Out of 10 available colorways, I selected five colors that promise the highest strike rates: Nude Gochujang, Watermelon, Watermelon Fabric, Military Green, and Gray. Each lure is neatly individually packaged; interestingly, Haedong designates them by numbers such as #1 and #2 rather than standard color names.

When it comes to foolproof color combinations that guarantee at least decent results regardless of conditions, I immediately think of Nude Gochujang and Military Green. Adding Watermelon and a natural baitfish shade rounds out a foolproof arsenal. In cuttlefish and octopus fishing, using the color combination shown below as your base rotation and adjusting as conditions dictate will yield the best results.

Let's take a closer look at the Nude Gochujang model. While Nude Gochujang egis are offered by diverse tackle manufacturers, the main body tint is fairly similar across brands. Here, the fin feathers appear somewhat fuller, and most remarkably, it incorporates a high-grade swivel mechanism unseen on other standard egis.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjEwMDVfMTY0/MDAxNzkxMTk0NzgwODg3.UWWlNFTMLq7NseWG-J98HEFmp05XSadRGY8mRUPmlbEg.Gd9-3QK0U1yaLpIMzoEWT4zowM7mi1jmHr-6qZKL2uIg.JPEG/SE-9d83d5ad-bfd8-11f1-8edd-abc6cfa2c407.jpg?type=w800)

The swivel rotates in two distinct axes: in addition to standard rotational spinning, an articulated circular ring provides additional free pivoting. Thanks to this dual articulation, the egi maintains an unhindered horizontal posture under any current condition without binding—undoubtedly its greatest distinctive engineering advantage.

Let's first test the glow-in-the-dark luminous charge. Even photographed in dim lighting rather than total darkness, the phosphorescence is impressive. The green and natural fabric lines in particular boast intense, vibrant glow.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjEwMDVfMTE2/MDAxNzkxMTk0NTQ4MDI5.XiNWvBLLi2VN8M1Qvg1d6eJjdWYhdumYzAs3XnVAEwYg.YCsl1PSSh9uQKlBa8P443jXPBb5TjkbzcN97iuip2nEg.JPEG/SE-9d7556b0-bfd8-11f1-8edd-2fb00b0fcec6.jpg?type=w800)

Taking an even closer shot reveals excellent body glow, but notably, the tinsel on the fins also carries strong luminescence. This detail clearly sets it apart from budget egis.

And nowadays, UV Keimura reaction is essential. Under UV light, #2 Watermelon, #7 Military Green, and #10 Gray display distinct Keimura fluorescence. While #1 Nude Gochujang and #6 Fabric Watermelon are listed without Keimura in the catalog, they appear to give off a subtle UV response as well.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjEwMDVfMjAz/MDAxNzkxMTk0NzAwMDYx.pSO6P6KXshzkQvz8LV5jFHsl65njp3abzI84DdBa204g.GRI-i-orBGkspsN1jOa6fplRDga2Bf1DVOTDpXJnm00g.JPEG/SE-9d764111-bfd8-11f1-8edd-6d2a44865bbb.jpg?type=w800)

I put the ROI BB egi to the test on an Incheon morning boat charter. For roughly half the trip, I ran a tandem rig pairing Nude Gochujang near the sinker and Military Green on the dropper branch, and the reaction was stellar. Both octopus and cuttlefish pounced aggressively, confirming its formidable appeal firsthand.
