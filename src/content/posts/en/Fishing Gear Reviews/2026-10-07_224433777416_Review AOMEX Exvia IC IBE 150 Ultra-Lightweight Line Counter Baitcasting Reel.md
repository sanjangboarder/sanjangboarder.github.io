Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

The peak season for webfoot octopus is gradually winding down, and October has arrived, putting cuttlefish center stage. Unlike webfoot octopus fishing, cuttlefish trips often venture out to deeper waters, making it much more convenient to have larger line capacity and the ability to check your water depth.

AOMEX has released a brand-new, genuinely lightweight line counter reel for the 2026 season, so I picked one up to take a close, detailed look.

The product name is the Exvia IC Baitcasting Reel, and its model number is IBE 150.

Let's take a meticulous look starting from the product packaging. Delivery was swift, and it arrived securely and safely packaged.

To my knowledge, as of October 2026, this might be the lightest digital line counter reel ever made. Looking at the prominent contenders that come to mind—the Daiwa Tierra AIR IC at 165g, Shimano Barchetta double-handle at 195g, Creman Little Giant DLC at 186g, and Doyo Liger DLC at 161g—they all exceed 160g. Even searching through products imported directly from China via AliExpress, I couldn't find a single model weighing under 160g.

As you can see in the specifications on the box below, the AOMEX Exvia IC reel weighs a staggering "158g." As of today, it is truly the lightest line counter baitcasting reel available.

On most DLC or counter reels, the depth-displaying LCD is typically positioned right above the level wind. Uniquely, however, this model features its LCD screen positioned on the side.

In other words, even though the digital LCD was moved to the side, the reel body itself hasn't grown bulkier—the display is simply ingeniously integrated into an optimal spot.

The depth-reading LCD incorporates an ultra-lightweight display with a high-brightness panel so it remains easily readable even under intense outdoor sunlight. The screen dimensions are generously sized at 20mm by 14mm, providing excellent visibility.

There is another notable distinction compared to other baitcasting reels: the magnetic brake has been omitted entirely. In reality, a magnetic brake is rarely essential for vertical boat fishing, so omitting it to achieve maximum weight reduction and a compact body is a very welcome design choice. Instead, there is a dial resembling a magnetic brake knob, which functions as an indicator for the PE line gauge spooled on the reel. It supports markings for PE 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, and 2.0, covering virtually all braid sizes commonly used in Korean boat fishing.

Naturally, it features a drag clicker that produces a crisp, pleasing audible sound. Rotating the star drag and mechanical brake knob also yields satisfying micro-clicks, showing refinement distinctly superior to entry-level baitcasters. You can check it out in the clip.

I paired it with an ultra-lightweight octopus rod purchased from AliExpress, and the reel's dark-toned colorway matched the rod very nicely. Having the depth counter was handy because it let me monitor water depth as the boat shifted across octopus spots. While depth in typical webfoot octopus or cuttlefish fishing isn't overly deep and knowing exact figures isn't strictly mandatory, having precise real-time depth measurements will prove extraordinarily useful for fisheries like spear squid (ika metal) or red sea bream tie rubber.
