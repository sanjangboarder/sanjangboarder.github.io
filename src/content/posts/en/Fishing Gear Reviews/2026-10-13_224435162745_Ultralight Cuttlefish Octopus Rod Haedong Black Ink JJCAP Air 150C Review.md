Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

Haedong has released a new ultralight 1-piece boat rod for webfoot octopus and cuttlefish for the 2026 autumn season. Having used it on the water myself, I would like to introduce what kind of rod it is. In terms of pricing, it can be found in the 170,000 KRW range based on Naver Shopping lowest prices.

Many different ultralight octopus and cuttlefish rods have hit the market this year, so we will focus closely on what distinguishing features set this model apart.

First off, delivery arrived very promptly, and a hard rod case is included as standard.

One-piece rods require substantially longer cases compared to 2-piece models. Very few ultralight rods supply such cases by default—they are usually sold as paid extras—but the Haedong Black Ink JJCAP Air 150C comes packaged with a hard case. Considering that a separate hard case typically costs around 20,000 to 30,000 KRW, this is a fantastic inclusion.

Without the rod saver counterweight installed, the balance point rests just ahead of the reel seat, which is reasonably well-balanced.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjEwMDVfNzUg/MDAxNzkxMTk0MTkwMTMz._1-2wS4Y6gdcjw-FYN6WWyw4Rtyex0u6zXMKT-Kue8gg.LqAs9fPqU0ua0qpQCQJd8Jkq_fdWWFSF8pkys44de4sg.JPEG/SE-5e92e346-bfd5-11f1-8edd-f9acd0e382f2.jpg?type=w800)

When installing the rod saver weight, the center of gravity shifts slightly behind the reel seat center. Once a baitcasting reel is mounted, the balance point aligns almost perfectly with the reel seat, offsetting any minor added weight with dramatically improved leverage and balance.

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjEwMDVfMzcg/MDAxNzkxMTk0MjA1OTgz.rUb9lDM_QSnHQctanUAaGG3ATfQR61jG1ESdP3DW8qYg.pxxPbWl1X4snMtrwOlxBeIH3FO4BNyVw1Z6XRxA2MFcg.JPEG/SE-5e941bc7-bfd5-11f1-8edd-4dabb1619735.jpg?type=w800)

The action curve features an 8.5:1.5 taper. In my cuttlefish and octopus trips, I operated it with size 14 to 16 sinkers, and the tactile sensitivity was superb at these weights. Looking at the bend, up to size 20 seems quite comfortable, with size 25 likely representing the practical upper limit.

Also, regarding the hard case: on a trip coaching beginners, I fit up to four rods inside! Being built to accommodate a 150 cm 1-piece rod, it offers generous internal volume, allowing you to transport at least four octopus/cuttlefish rods at once with tremendous convenience.

On a recent Incheon half-day boat trip targeting octopus and cuttlefish, I used the Haedong Black Ink JJCAP Air as my primary rod. Needless to say, the featherlight handling produced zero fatigue; with a combined rod-and-reel weight around 150 g, the effortless handling was immediate.

I fished with a size 16 sinker, and the tactile signal was unmistakably clear even when an octopus barely placed a single tentacle on the egi. When cuttlefish pulled, the transmission was astonishingly vivid. In coastal inner bays with water depths under 30 meters, pairing it with size 14 to 20 sinkers appears to be the sweet spot.

Compared to typical ultralight rods that compromise on flimsy components—such as questionable guide quality or truncated rear grips—this model completely resolves those flaws while delivering an attractive, refined aesthetic.
