### 1. Bare Rod Weight & Balance Test

Weighing it on a digital scale, Section 1 (Tip) came out to **7.3g**, Section 2 (Butt) to **38.1g**, and the total rod weight measured **45.4g**! Although slightly heavier than the manufacturer's claimed 37g, hitting a **45g class ultra-light weight** for around 50,000 KRW (approx. $40 USD) is astonishing.

As for action taper, the seller describes it as 8:2, but in my hands, it feels closer to a **stiff 8.5:1.5 medium-heavy action**. It is stiffer than a true 9:1 fast rod, offering a crisp backbone that will feel optimal when using **#12 to #16 sinkers** on West Coast charter boats.

The reel seat and rear carbon grip ergonomics are virtually identical! The only slight difference is that the reel seat on the Eustass sits slightly further forward.

Furthermore, while the OSLO 39g uses 11 spiral guides, the **AliExpress Eustass L150 features 13 Fuji guides**, offering tighter guide spacing and a more stable blank bend curve under load.

​

While the OSLO 39g has a softer, flexible tip action, the Eustass L150 blank is stiffer (8.5:1.5 medium-heavy). Because I personally prefer stiffer blank actions and high sensitivity from tight guide layouts, I actually prefer the Eustass L150. Considering the **5-fold price gap**, I was honestly shocked.

---

​

### Conclusion

The single remaining unverified factor is long-term blank durability on heavy octopus. The manufacturer claims to use T1100G carbon technology. If blank durability holds up on the water, this rod could completely dominate the budget squid rod market this season!

​

Using AliExpress sale coupons or card discounts allows you to grab this rod right around **50,000 KRW (~$40 USD)**. Even at 60,000 to 70,000 KRW, its cost-to-performance value is overwhelming.
