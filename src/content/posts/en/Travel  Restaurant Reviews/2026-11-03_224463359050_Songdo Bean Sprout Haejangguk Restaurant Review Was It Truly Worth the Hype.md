Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

​

Last weekend, right after wrapping up a morning half-day boat fishing trip for webfoot octopus and cuttlefish, I headed over to the Old Songdo Resort restaurant district for lunch to visit a widely famous bean sprout hangover soup (kongnamul haejangguk) restaurant. Whenever I had driven past previously, it was always jammed with cars and bustling with people. While bean sprout soup isn't normally my top personal preference, my companions were eager to try it, so off we went haha.

​

Equipped with parking spaces in front, directly across the street, and a dedicated lot just a brief walk away, it turns out this was a beloved local institution that perhaps only I was unfamiliar with. We arrived around 11:30 AM on Sunday; luckily, there were open parking spots and, perhaps owing to the rainy weather, we were seated immediately without waiting in line.

The table layout is fairly tightly packed, but with many diligent waitstaff on hand, table turnover appeared remarkably fast. Customers promptly finish, exit, and new patrons sit down in rapid succession... While it is clearly an established, older restaurant, the operational system felt highly streamlined and efficient.

The menu starts with standard bean sprout haejangguk as the base, climbing up to around 13,000 KRW as specialty ingredients are added. I ordered the Kimchi Squid Haejangguk, my wife ordered the Oyster Bean Sprout Haejangguk, and another member of our party chose the classic base version.

Four basic side dishes are served, but there is a distinctive highlight: a generous bowl of finely shredded fish cakes (odeng). The restaurant's posted guide suggests scooping a generous handful of fish cakes directly into the piping-hot soup, wrapping bean sprouts and fish cake strips together with kimchi. Additionally, fresh salted baby shrimp (saeujeot) and diced Cheongyang hot peppers are provided for custom seasoning.

The bowl contains plenty of tender, plump squid pieces. What sets this place apart from typical bean sprout soup houses is that despite the generous volume of sprouts, their crisp, crunchy texture remains vividly intact. It seems likely that fresh bean sprouts are placed into the bowl right before scalding hot broth is poured over them just prior to serving.

Because I don't possess profound connoisseur insights into bean sprout soup, setting an absolute benchmark is tricky; however, even factoring in that I rarely seek out this dish, I thoroughly enjoyed my meal. The crisp texture of the bean sprouts was memorable, and eating with the suggested fishcake combination was novel and refreshing. If there had been a long waiting line, I might have opted for somewhere else, but entering without a wait makes it well worth a visit.

​

By my personal standards, I rate this restaurant 3.8 out of 5.0 stars. Genuine lovers of bean sprout hangover soup would likely award it an even higher score, but this reflects my honest assessment.

​

In Old Songdo (near the former Songdo Resort area), there are numerous restaurants with several decades of continuous history. Surviving for so long in business usually signifies a true culinary landmark with a dedicated following, and this restaurant fits that mold. If you enjoy authentic bean sprout soup, you won't regret stopping by.
