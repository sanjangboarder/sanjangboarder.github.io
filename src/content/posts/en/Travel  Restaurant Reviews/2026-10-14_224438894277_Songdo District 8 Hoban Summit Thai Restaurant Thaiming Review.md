Hello, this is Sanjangboarder.

A new Thai restaurant recently opened right in front of my home, so I dropped by for a meal. Lately, I have been craving Southeast Asian flavors, and between Vietnamese and Thai cooking, Thai dishes generally suit my palate a bit better. While Thai spots can be found around town, they rarely fell along my regular routes, so having one open right in my neighborhood naturally caught my attention.

The restaurant is situated on the 1st floor of the commercial arcade at Hoban Summit Songdo in Songdo District 8. Please refer to the map below.

Occupying a single commercial unit, the restaurant is cozy and modest in size. The menu primarily centers on classic Thai staples.

Because it is a single-unit shop, dining space inside is fairly compact. While many Thai restaurants opt for a slightly rustic street-stall vibe to evoke an authentic locale, this place is decorated in quite a sleek, clean, modern style. However, the number of tables is limited.

When ordering noodles, pickled sides and fish sauce are served automatically. The pickles are crisp and pleasantly tangy, cutting through any oily richness.

First up was Kuay Teow Nuea, a classic Thai beef rice noodle soup. While sharing surface similarities with Vietnamese pho, it carries distinct differences, particularly the bold depth lent by fish sauce.

<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjhfMTQ0/MDAxNzkwNTc3NjA0MjY2.ktZcHLjq8LGfj-mQdmT-7M5S261RQ4ud3WV-ckpeMd0g.OdVceZItKtWqmj-gdM31WhD5RYkLoF5duZU4X32x7JIg.JPEG/900_20260913_151235.jpg?type=w800" />
</div>

Fresh cilantro is included. The broth boasts a deep, dark color reminiscent of galbitang (Korean beef short rib soup) and delivers a concentrated stock base. The meat topping consists of sliced beef brisket (usamgyeop), meaning there is a noticeable portion of fat; diners who prefer leaner cuts might find their opinions divided.

<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjhfMjY5/MDAxNzkwNTc3NjA2MDk1.bHEi94gvOL5FGF6S1vZeJ3YvnTKw0iL6_JiJ3oP9lugg.8Wf0vbLNVAH_l_2VaFgIvsC6gbc-MzO0BinDwNfNsTQg.JPEG/900_20260913_151237.jpg?type=w800" />
</div>

Two dipping sauces are provided on the side for the beef, which paired quite well with the meat.

<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjhfMzUg/MDAxNzkwNTc3NjAzNjQy.jV-uMeSRsSNbEA3CV0YdNidAOzY_SaxBGjIGBRcJG-0g.ViIRxAy651wwUJZx0zZPi2uj36vL77FnlPSQT1wkqiwg.JPEG/900_20260913_151247%280%29.jpg?type=w800" />

The noodles are thin rice noodles with a texture somewhat reminiscent of flat kalguksu. Rather than an extraordinary dish that demands an out-of-the-way pilgrimage, it reliably hits the essential standard. Priced at 11,000 KRW, it functions well as a dependable staple option.

The next dish was Pad Krapow Moo Saap (Thai basil pork rice bowl). Personally, this suited my taste buds substantially better than the noodles. It was priced at 13,000 KRW.

<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjhfMTg2/MDAxNzkwNTc3NjA3MTgx.uNwajvunCicYqejPaKEHGdM3RTcREI1T7BFG3durJ9Eg.Qxf9AUuYm8GefCszQR0Q4A5TLkpzJdyRvgXBDqp2ml8g.JPEG/900_20260913_151332.jpg?type=w800" />
</div>

This dish features minced pork wok-fried with fragrant Thai herbs and seasonings. There was no gamy odor, and the vegetables retained a crisp, lively bite, making it an accessible, delicious dish that almost anyone will enjoy.

It comes topped with a single fried egg, impeccably shallow-fried with crispy edges and a luscious runny yolk. Easily one of the best-cooked fried eggs I have encountered recently. Combining the warm rice, savory basil pork stir-fry, and rich runny yolk created a fantastic harmony of flavors.

Our final choice was Gai Pad Med Mamuang, ordered because it reminded me of Chinese kkangpunggi. It can be understood as a Thai-style stir-fried chicken tossed in a sweet and savory sauce, making it an ideal drinking accompaniment.

<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjhfODIg/MDAxNzkwNTc3NjA1OTA1.lQaVVpgYHxqTZ0JaEhZBXnr79GWgRgmuunJ3b0rkhNsg.qo4qYnwuT9waDPZyeIW3VzEhi1jEthjGuhbB_7q_j8sg.JPEG/900_20260913_152408.jpg?type=w800" />
</div>

Fried chili peppers are tossed in, adding a surprisingly delightful kick and crispness. Another nice touch was the generous cashews, which were great to pick out and savor. Rather than a meal on its own, it pairs wonderfully alongside an ice-cold beer.

<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA5MjhfMTc5/MDAxNzkwNTc3NjAzNjg1.FQRLB_j8g7tCWj2MckXawRSFo-w7GgdogAUQD9WPCfQg.FXPIS0VD10-Rqs5puajvhoqrtEzM8oJvBN-iDCW0MOcg.JPEG/900_20260913_152412.jpg?type=w800" />
</div>

On this outing, three of us shared these three dishes. I particularly enjoyed the pork basil rice and the Thai cashew chicken, whereas the noodles were decent but nothing extraordinary. With other items on the menu remaining unexplored, I will likely return for another visit.

While perhaps not a destination spot worth traveling from afar to Songdo District 8, it is a great neighborhood dining option that local residents can comfortably visit for a satisfying meal.

Thank you.
