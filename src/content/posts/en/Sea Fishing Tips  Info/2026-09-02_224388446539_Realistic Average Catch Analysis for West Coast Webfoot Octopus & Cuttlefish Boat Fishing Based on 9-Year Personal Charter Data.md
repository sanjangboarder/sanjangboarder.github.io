### 2. AI Analytical Insights: Expected Average Catches

Analyzing my 48 charter trips using Google Gemini Notebook AI models revealed that my average expected catch per trip lands at **approximately 100 cephalopods** on general squid boats, and **57 cuttlefish** on dedicated cuttlefish charters.

​

​

* **Tide Analysis:** Catches remained remarkably stable from Neap Tides (조금) through 9-mul (9물). However, during **10-mul to 13-mul (dying spring tides)**, catches plummeted by **-36%**. This occurs because water turbidity peaks right after 9-mul, creating muddy water conditions that hurt visibility and active biting.

​

<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA4MjNfMjUw/MDAxNzg3NDYwNjAwMTc1.drZJUeo-OVIq9JbAWyo7lpiah-Nz3UW8ezASV5ZbkIIg.W1sKeQlNJRxQa-9y98eWJDgec8l6SzszgVlGX6rcgz8g.PNG/image.png?type=w800" />

​

* **Weather Penalty Impact:** Compared to clear days, rain reduced catches by **-9%**, and strong wind reduced catches by **-18%**. When strong wind was accompanied by **heavy sea swells**, catches crashed by **-66%**! Heavy swells cause severe boat rolling, losing bottom contact sensitivity, increasing seasickness downtime, and halving total catches.

### Conclusion

Looking at my raw data, there were plenty of days where I failed to hit even 50 fish due to heavy swells, freshwater dam releases, or mentoring beginner anglers. Realizing that the true long-term average lands right around **100 fish per trip** was eye-opening.

For veteran anglers taking 5+ trips per year, an average of 150 fish per trip is realistic. For beginners, setting a initial target of **50 fish**, and working up to **100+ fish after 2-3 years**, makes the journey far more rewarding.

I have set my personal 2026 seasonal goal to **1,000 total cephalopods** across my planned 11 charter trips!
