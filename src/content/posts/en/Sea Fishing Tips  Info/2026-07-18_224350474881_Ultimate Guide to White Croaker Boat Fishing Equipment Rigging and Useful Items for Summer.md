With the peak season for white croaker (*Pennahia argentata*) boat fishing—one of the most popular summer sea fishing genres—underway, I have put together a comprehensive guide on essential gear, rigging setups, and helpful accessories. If you want to experience energetic bites and fill your cooler with abundance along Korea's West Coast, use this guide to plan your trip.

​

When targeting white croaker in the summer, choosing the right strategy is key. Personally, I prefer targeting "Jumbo White Croaker" over sheer quantity. While personal preference dictates which style is more enjoyable, if you prefer high-volume quantity, ports like Hongwon Port or Daecheon Port are ideal. If you want to target trophy-sized jumbo croakers ranging from 25cm to 40cm, I highly recommend Mageompo Port.

The white croaker season typically begins in June, starting at Hongwon Port and gradually moving northward. The peak season runs throughout July and August. By September, most charter boats switch to webfoot octopus and cuttlefish fishing, effectively marking the end of the white croaker season. As a rule of thumb, fish sizes tend to improve later in the season.

​

The pattern in 2026 has been slightly different. Normally, high-volume catches at Hongwon Port would be peaking by now, but the season has started a bit later this year. High-volume catches are expected to begin in earnest toward late July.

🎣 1. Basic Tackle Setup (Rod / Reel / Line)

White croaker fishing does not require specialized, dedicated gear. Beginners can easily participate using existing light boat fishing tackle:

**Rod:** You can use flatfish downshot, live-bait (Oesujil), or octopus rods. Slow jigging or light jigging rods work just as well. On the West Coast, sinker weights typically range from #30 to #50, so any rod capable of handling this weight range will suffice.

🔗 [JS Company Liberty Ocean Flatfish & Rockfish Boat Rod](https://naver.me/GmpVLFLr)

​

**Reel:** Baitcasting reels with a 5:1 or 6:1 gear ratio are ideal. Jumbo croaker spots sometimes sit in deeper waters (40m–60m depth). In those cases, using a compact electric reel makes retrieval effortless, though it is not strictly mandatory.

🔗 [Doyo Urano Inshore G4 Baitcasting Reel](https://naver.me/x9VjPIuk)

​

**Line:** PE braided line around Size 1.0 (0.8 to 1.2) is recommended. Thinner line cuts through currents better and reduces line drag. You can use the line already spooled on your octopus, flatfish, or taibarareels.

🪝 2. Essential Rigs and Baits

Tackle choices and bait presentation dictate your overall catch rate more than your rod or reel.

​

**Recommended Rigs:** Standard multi-hook rockfish rigs are widely used. In particular, the **Baekkyung Damping Rockfish Rig (Small)** is optimized for white croaker. Avoid rigs with green plastic spreader arms as they tangle easily. Carry 5 to 10 spare rigs in case of bottom snags.

​

**Sinkers:** #40 sinkers are the standard baseline. Adjust down to #30 in lighter currents or up to #50 in deeper waters.

🔗 [Rotational Downshot Sinker #40 (4-pack)](https://naver.me/5o08MDKR)

​

**Hooks:** When targeting jumbo croakers, large rockfish hooks (#22 to #24, minimum #20) are best. Small hooks get swallowed deep into the stomach, wasting precious time removing them. Larger hooks set cleanly on the lip for quick release. Double-hook rigs excel in volume spots, while single-hook live-bait rigs work best for jumbo croaker spots.

🔗 [Baekkyung Boat Rockfish Damping Rig BK-400 (Small)](https://naver.me/xaTvckpG)

​

**Bait:** Fresh lugworms (blue/red sandworms) produce the highest bite rate. Thread the worm up over the hook shank and leave a long tail dangling. When targeting jumbo croakers, hook the worm whole without cutting it. Alternative baits include canned silkworm pupae, Berkley Gulp Sandworms, and salted sandworms.

🔗 [Berkley Gulp! Sandworm 2-inch](https://naver.me/5Enfyer1)

💡 Practical Fishing Tips

1. **Fast Hookset:** White croakers bite sharply. Keep line tension tight and set the hook immediately upon feeling a bite. Quick hooksets are vital in high-volume spots, whereas jumbo croakers allow a slightly more patient hookset.

2. **Bait Presentation:** In high-volume spots, trim sandworms short to cover just the hook point. In jumbo croaker spots, thread one whole worm to present a large, tempting target.

3. **Field Processing:** Scale, salt, and pack your fish into heavy-duty plastic bags during spot transitions to prevent fish odor from permeating your cooler.
