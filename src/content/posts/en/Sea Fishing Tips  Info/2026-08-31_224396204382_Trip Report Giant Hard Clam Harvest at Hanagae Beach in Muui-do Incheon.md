​

At Hanagae Beach, water recedes very quickly, and incoming tides rush in even faster! You must begin your return walk the instant lowest neap tide hits.

My buddy Kim had gone out targeting swimming crabs an hour before low tide. However, Coast Guard officers on hovercrafts patrolled deep mudflats warning harvesters about dangerous incoming tides. Prioritizing safety, he returned to join me for clam harvesting.

​

### 2. Using Dragging Rakes ("Geurae") & Uncovering Monster-Sized Clams

Once visual clams disappeared post-drainage, we deployed our shoulder dragging rakes called **Geurae (그레)**.

As you pull the rake across sand flats, you feel a distinct *"Tick!"* click when it strikes a buried shell. You immediately dig that spot with a hand hoe to pull out Hard Clams. Working in 2-person teams—one pulling the rake to locate clams and the other digging—doubles efficiency!

### 3. Purging Sand (Haegam) Tips & Conclusion

Because I had work the next morning, I packed enough for two family meals into a container with clean sea water. Hard Clams purge sand quickly; keeping them submerged in clean ocean water during the drive home completely clears sand grit by next day.
