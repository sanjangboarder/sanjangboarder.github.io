**1. Basic Fishing Tackle (Rod, Reel, Line)**

**Rod:** While dedicated sea bass Oesujil rods exist, light jigging rods with a soft tip section and a strong backbone are ideal. Soft, responsive tips make bite detection significantly easier. If you don't own a dedicated Oesujil rod, you can substitute existing flatfish downshot, rockfish, or octopus boat rods.


<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA3MThfMTMw/MDAxNzg0MzczMTEwMTk4.z8h8I6qrKMC9sbGDuvzMI-35uuPRwNOM15foTU9OHkgg.yt0jV3U6_Jr3ct8dGGghN_0uEZAkipcNawQdt-Xqypog.PNG/image.png?type=w800" />
</div>

**Baitcasting Reel:** Sea bass make explosive, powerful runs. Use a baitcasting reel with strong drag capability. Reels with drag ratings from 4-5kg (octopus reels) up to 8-9kg+ are usable, but higher drag capacity provides better control when hooking meter-class "trophy" sea bass. Lower gear ratios (around 5:1) offer smoother cranking power during heavy fights.

​

**Drag Adjustment:** Unlike snapper taibarareels (loose drag) or flatfish downshot reels (moderate drag), sea bass Oesujil requires a **firmly set drag**. Adjust the drag so the spool gives line only under a heavy, strong tug with two fingers. Allowing a large sea bass to freely run with a loose drag increases line wrap risks with neighboring anglers. **Never thumb the spool to stop a run**, as sudden shock loading will snap the braided line instantly.

<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA3MThfMjE0/MDAxNzg0MzczMTIxNzI5.DpQJvAQYIljlUuy1OMpOf4uHdOz6yFZs7Ly6M6tAd0sg.3woZm6KRVBlXrro_AhfiXQcfKOK6jpv0r6L1Lp_u1MYg.PNG/image.png?type=w800" />

**Main Line:** Braided PE lines between Size 1.2 and 3.0 are commonly used. While experienced anglers use PE 1.0 for better sensitivity, beginners should opt for PE 2.0 to 3.0 to prevent line breakage during snags or fights with large fish.

**2. Rigs & Weights**

Using pre-made commercial single-dropper rigs is far more convenient than tying custom rigs. Carry 5 to 10 spare rigs per trip. Avoid spreader-arm (pyeondae) rigs, as they tangle easily with adjacent anglers' lines.


<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA3MThfMjQg/MDAxNzg0MzczMTMyNDM4.6gSQTtUDj-czWl9k1B6L-iv3iS2SVREDbNsBE6WuSNAg.5vJ4TaLXwFjD1V7AM7EWHCUKz_5Klmu-wj9I7fZsbHkg.PNG/image.png?type=w800" />
</div>

**Hooks:** Use sea bass / live-bait hooks ranging from size #18 to #24. Hook size #18 works well early in the season, while #20 to #24 is recommended during peak season for larger fish.

​

**Sinkers:** Sinker weights range from #30 to #50. Always match your sinker weight to the captain's announcement before fishing to keep lines dropping at the same rate and avoid tangles.

**3. Essential Live Bait Care & Accessories**

Keeping live shrimp lively and fresh is the key factor in live-bait sea bass fishing.

* **Bait Equipment:** Bring a battery-powered air pump (aerator), a small bait net, and a personal bait bucket/livewell.
* **Bait Quantity:** Prepare at least 30 to 40+ live shrimp per angler. Discard dead shrimp immediately and refresh your hook with lively bait.

​

**💡 Essential Field Tactics & Etiquette**

1. **Keep Bait off the Bottom:** Sea bass are not bottom-dwelling species. Dragging your sinker along reefs or the seabed will spook feeding schools. Touch bottom briefly, then immediately reel up 0.5m to 1m+ as instructed by the captain.

</div>

2. **Correct Shrimp Rigging:** Insert the hook point just below the horn on the head, avoiding the dark brain spot, and push it up centered through the top. Proper centering prevents the shrimp from spinning unnaturally in the current.


<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA3MThfMjcw/MDAxNzg0MzczMTU2MTg4.dSutQXJ4eSIYBj2E8NdPrZ7a1vK3d1pFU5yxYifpcjEg.9L85IYgahyC6pL8y7YZ5BuHlegSz0v4uM83svxgu2U4g.PNG/image.png?type=w800" />
</div>

3. **No Violent Hooksets:** When you feel initial taps, **do not set the hook forcefully**. Wait patiently until the rod loads down as the fish swallows the bait, then smoothly lift the rod tip while maintaining steady reeling.


<div class="single-image">
<img src="https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA3MThfMTcg/MDAxNzg0MzczMTkzNzI2.T5EtfSsvcBqaLgz9IsdEl84xDJkCQ8UpOixbBdsqOLcg.jTjyPee3lSfBHLCt_dKvp5DqjAAiwoo7vO5qajdu39Mg.PNG/image.png?type=w800" />
</div>

4. **Landing Etiquette:** Keep the sea bass's head underwater until a net is ready. Lifting the head above the surface triggers head-shaking behavior that often throws the hook.
