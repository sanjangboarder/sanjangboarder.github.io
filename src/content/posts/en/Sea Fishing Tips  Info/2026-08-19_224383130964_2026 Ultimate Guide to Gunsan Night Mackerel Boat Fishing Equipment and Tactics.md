**Selecting Trip Dates & Tides**

The midsummer month of July through early autumn in September is the peak season. However, rather than picking dates based strictly on calendar months, target times right after spring tides (사리 물때) when water temperatures stabilize and clear up. Most importantly, night mackerel catches fluctuate heavily depending on daily conditions, so checking up-to-the-minute daily charter catch logs right before booking is critical.

​

**Equipment Options: Rods & Reels**

* **Rods:** Light Jigging rods, Taibarareel rods, or Ika Metal rods with flexible tip sections capable of handling 60g to 100g metal jigs without bouncing fish off the hook.
* **Reels:** **Digital Line-Counter Baitcasting Reels** are strongly recommended. Mackerel move constantly in suspended layers, so knowing your exact line counter depth to share with fellow anglers is the single most important key to holding the school under boat lights.

​

**Bait & Terminal Tackle Setup**

​

**- Live / Cut Bait**

While saury strips or squid strips bought from tackle shops work fine, filleting small juvenile mackerel (called *Godori*) caught directly on deck to use as fresh bait yields by far the highest strike rates. Bringing a sharp filleting knife onboard to prepare bait strips is a great tip.

​

**- Multi-Hook Dropper Rigs**

When using live cut bait, running a multi-hook dropper rig alongside your metal jig is highly recommended. Lure anglers also use cut bait! While you can fish with just a metal jig, relying solely on metal lures when others are dropping live bait puts you at a disadvantage. However, **never use cheap 5 to 10-hook inshore sabiki rigs!** Haha. Those thin rigs are designed for shore casting; under the strain of multiple 35cm+ mackerel, they snap lines, tangle neighbors, and ruin deck space. Use heavy-duty 2 to 3-hook rigs built with thick trunk lines and short branch lines to prevent line coil tangles.

​

**- Underwater Attraction Lights**

While optional, attaching an underwater attraction light definitely gives a sensory boost that makes fish stick around longer. Attach eyeball-shaped lights near the metal jig head, or inline squid lights along your shock leader.

**- Ice Box / Coolers**

Like all fishing, everyone imagines coming back home with a full cooler when night mackerel fishing. But that only happens during legendary feeding frenzies; on average days, a **24-liter cooler** is plenty. Many anglers bring up to 40-liter coolers just in case of a massive catch, but if everyone brings giant coolers over 40L, walking space on deck becomes extremely cramped and uncomfortable. Limit your cooler to 40 liters maximum, or bring a smaller one.

**- Forceps & Pliers**

Assist hooks, treble hooks, and quad hooks attached to metal jigs combined with thrashing mackerel, horse mackerel, and toothy Spanish mackerel can be extremely dangerous. **Never try to unhook them with your bare hands!** Knock Spanish mackerel unconscious immediately, and use surgical forceps to safely extract hooks from mackerel and horse mackerel. Accidents frequently happen while unhooking fish on night boats, so forceps are a mandatory safety tool.

**Night Mackerel Operational Tactics**

The single most critical factor in night mackerel fishing is locating the active depth layer. Honestly, if you master this, 70% of your trip is already a success. Mackerel, horse mackerel, and Spanish mackerel move up and down depth layers freely. Once the active depth of the moving school is identified on deck, everyone can position their metal jigs or live bait at the exact same line counter depth, keeping the school pinned beneath the vessel without scattering.

​

![](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNjA8MDRfMzAg/MDAxNzg1ODIxMDE1ODQy.Z8ZJlT2bt4n4gjvrS4VMxlvZKHSp1SpJJU0j1FRlEjsg.XWDNS5VTV7ov-efswLyJFe_LEgygvIswAQWPksszrukg.PNG/image.png?type=w800)

​

This is precisely why boat captains emphasize bringing line-counter reels, and just like squid fishing, teamwork across anglers on deck is vital.

​

Jerking, short-jerking, and tensioned falling are the primary actions to trigger bites. Rotate through different actions to find the pattern of the day. Sharing active patterns with friends and neighboring anglers so everyone gets bites together is very important. If someone nearby is secretly hooking up continuously, keeping a eagle eye on their retrieve action, metal jig color, and weight to copy their setup is a smart tactical response.
